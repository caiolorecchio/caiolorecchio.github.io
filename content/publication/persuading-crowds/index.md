abstract: "I study dynamic persuasion when agents learn from each other. A self-interested principal commits to an information policy; agents act in sequence, observing private signals and predecessors' actions. Since past actions can reveal private information, social learning moves beliefs beyond the principal's control, even when he is silent. I show his value is bounded by the persuasion value under unobservable actions, which he attains if and only if an immediate cascade-inducing policy does. Otherwise, under conditions on agents' private information, I characterize the optimal policy: each disclosure is less informative, yet society learns more over time. Unlike for a benevolent planner, patience makes social learning less valuable to the principal."
