abstract: "This paper studies the social value of public information in cooperative settings. It establishes a novel link between the welfare effects of public information and its potential to destabilize socially desirable cooperative arrangements. When public signals do not destabilize any cooperative arrangement that is stable without them, public information cannot decrease welfare. In transferable utility games where stability is defined via the core, public information has no impact on welfare. By contrast, when public signals alter which cooperative arrangements are stable, public information can reduce welfare. Departing from the traditional focus on risk-sharing, we show that the social value of public information can be negative even when individuals are risk neutral. The underlying source of this welfare loss is a fundamental tension between efficiency and stability."
